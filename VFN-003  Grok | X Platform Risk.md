X is changing. Your business needs to know what's happening.

Three things happened to X in the last 60 days. None of them were announced together. Together they tell you something important about where the platform is heading.

X is not falling apart. But it is a different platform than it was two months ago. The bot removal, the Communities shutdown, and the EU fine all landed in the same window. Any one of those would matter on its own. All three together mean the platform a lot of businesses built their distribution on is under real structural pressure and making aggressive moves to deal with it. ## Development 1 | Bot Removal

On April 9, Nikita Bier, who runs product for X, said publicly that the platform was suspending 208 bot accounts per minute and that the rate was still growing. At that pace, you're looking at roughly 300,000 accounts removed per day. This has been running continuously for weeks. Bier had already said in March that more than half his product team was focused entirely on spam mitigation. In October 2025, they pulled 1.7 million bots in a single operation. What's happening now is faster and it's ongoing, not a one-time sweep.

The problem that actually affects you is that legitimate accounts are getting flagged. Verified follower counts went up while overall follower numbers dropped. That tells you the detection is working at a broad level but it is not precise. Social media managers, marketers, and anyone running audience research through third-party tools need to pay attention. If your follower count dropped this month with no explanation, this is the likely cause. If you use third-party apps to access X, those accounts are being flagged as bot-like behavior.

## Development 2 | Communities Shutdown

X announced on April 22 that Communities is shutting down. The final date is May 30. They originally set May 6 but extended it after admins pushed back. Bier's numbers are hard to argue with. Less than 0.4% of users were actually using Communities. But that feature was responsible for 80% of the spam reports, scams, and malware across the entire platform. It was also eating half the product team's time in some weeks. From a resource standpoint, keeping it didn't make sense.

The replacement is XChat, which launched as a standalone iOS app on April 24. Group chats currently hold 500 members with a target of 1,000 soon. That cap is a real problem for anyone with a large community. iShowSpeed noted his community had over 100,000 members. There is no current migration path for that scale. Beyond the size issue, XChat group chats are a fundamentally different experience. Communities had asynchronous feeds and moderation tools. XChat is real-time group messaging. They serve different purposes. If you built something in Communities, you have until May 30 and need a plan now.

*Sources: Reuters · TechCrunch · Engadget · Nikita Bier (X, Head of Product)*

## Development 3 | EU Regulatory Action

In December, the European Commission fined X €120 million, about $140 million. It was the first non-compliance fine ever issued under the Digital Services Act. Three violations: the blue checkmark is deceptive because anyone can pay for verified status without X confirming who they are; the ad repository lacks required transparency; and X blocked researchers from accessing public data.

X appealed in February and submitted proposed checkmark changes in March. The EU is reviewing those. The fine still had to be paid regardless. Additional remedies on the remaining violations were due April 28. If X doesn't comply, more penalties follow. This is not a settled matter. It is active regulatory supervision with escalation built in for businesses operating in EU markets.

## What This Means

X is not collapsing. But the platform most businesses were using 18 months ago is not the one running today. Follower counts are shifting because of the bot sweep. Communities are disappearing with no true equivalent. And the EU now has enforcement power over how X operates. None of this is speculative. All three are confirmed, sourced, and happening right now. If X is part of your distribution or research stack, this is a platform-risk window worth taking seriously.
